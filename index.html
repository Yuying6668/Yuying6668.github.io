Salamanca, Spain · 2020 – 2025

Beyond usage metrics: what users do, and how they feel

I moved to Spain for a PhD with a very business-relevant question: platforms measure how often and how long people use them, but do those numbers actually predict how users feel?

After five years, two survey studies and a three-wave panel, my answer was: mostly not.

What users do matters more than how long they stay, and how they feel shapes what they do next.
Researcher & Data Analyst University of Salamanca · Full-time · Oct 2020 – Jun 2025

Context

Behavioural research at the Audiovisual Content Observatory (OCA): how people use social platforms, and how that use relates to satisfaction and well-being.

What I did

  • Ran large-scale user behaviour studies end to end: survey design, data collection with external panel providers, cleaning, validation and documentation
  • Nationwide survey of 1,224 users: motivations explained satisfaction better than time- or frequency-based usage metrics
  • Three-wave panel of 1,671 participants to separate high-impact behaviours from misleading usage metrics, and to find which way effects run between how users feel and how they engage
  • Statistical modelling in SPSS and Mplus; presented findings to academic and industry audiences

Outcome

Behaviour-based metrics gave sharper user insight than conventional usage indicators: the same choice product teams face when picking engagement KPIs.

Trust Analyst Paavis · Part-time · Oct 2026 – present

Context

Paavis measures how trustworthy B2B companies look to buyers through their public digital presence. The method turns Mayer, Davis and Schoorman's ability–benevolence–integrity model into 75 indicators, 13 signals and 3 pillars, summed up in a 1–99 Net Trust Score.

What I did

  • Run Rapid inspections (42 indicators, website only, about an hour per company) and Full inspections (all 75 indicators plus a verification layer)
  • Verify claims against company registries, ad libraries, review platforms, LinkedIn and AI assistants, and record where every piece of evidence came from
  • Follow a reproducible protocol: anchored 1–5 scales, rule-based sampling, reverse-scored indicators, and flags for anything the evidence cannot settle
  • Contribute to client reports that rank signals by how much each would lift the score

Outcome

Evidence-based diagnoses that show a company which trust signals help, which hurt and where to invest first. In one Full inspection, fixing the three weakest signals would lift a client's score from 59 to about 77.
