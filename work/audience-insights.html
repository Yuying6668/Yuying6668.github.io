Film & TV audience insights: from survey data to release strategy

As a marketing data analyst at CC-Smart's film market research centre in Beijing, I worked on audience research for studios and broadcasters: a national cinema-goer survey, a pre-release study for a major fantasy film, and a data-driven assessment of a TV drama before it went on air.

01A national cinema survey

54cities covered
2,000+valid responses
Tier 1–2cities with the highest attendance
  • Helped design the questionnaire for a national survey of cinema audiences, and supervised fieldwork and data cleaning across 54 cities.
  • Validated questionnaires and coordinated field teams to keep data quality consistent between cities.
  • Found markedly higher cinema attendance in Tier-1 and Tier-2 cities, which fed into the client's expansion planning and promotion targeting.

02Pre-release study: Legend of the Demon Cat

Three months before Chen Kaige's fantasy epic Legend of the Demon Cat (2017) opened, the studio needed to know who was interested, what drew them in, and what could go wrong. I designed and analysed two offline questionnaires, one for general audiences and one for fans of the cast.

2,323general-audience respondents (weighted, from 3,494)
81.2%already knew the film
87.1%of those planned to see it in cinemas
  • Two audiences, two decisions. 93.2% of fans said the cast decided whether they would go, 44 points more than general viewers, who weighed story, genre and the director as well. Marketing needed two tracks, not one.
  • A positioning risk. 65.7% saw the film as fantasy, but over 30% expected a mystery or a romance and only 22.4% an art film. I recommended making the genre clear in all materials to avoid mismatched expectations.
  • The director was the strongest asset. Chen Kaige scored the highest expectation of the whole cast and crew (4.09 out of 5), with 96% awareness.

03After release: a published case

The film earned 529 million RMB and strong reviews, but less than its quality suggested. Together with a co-author, I analysed why in a chapter of the Blue Book of China's Film and TV Industry (2018): "Legend of the Demon Cat: Conflicts of Art and Market under Fantasy Features" (B.14). The analysis linked the box office to the same tension my pre-release data had flagged: an art-house ambition in a fantasy wrapper, released between two strong competitors.

04Pre-broadcast assessment: Abyss Walker

For Abyss Walker, a 42-episode undercover crime drama planned for Hunan TV's prime-time slot and online platforms, I was asked to predict interest and reception before broadcast.

1 · Market13 TV dramas & 14 web series in the genre
2 · Platform19 dramas in Hunan TV's prime-time slot
3 · Benchmark10 comparable titles, scored on topic, plot and cast
4 · AudienceDouban reviews, Baidu Index & Weibo data
  • The genre was scarce on TV (13 titles in 19 months) with decent reviews (average Douban 6.4) and an average rating of 0.97, which left room to grow.
  • Hunan TV's slot averaged a 2.87 rating but was dominated by romance and costume dramas, so a crime drama would stand out, with a young audience to win over.
  • Review mining showed what this audience punishes: slow plots and unnecessary romance subplots. That became my main content recommendation.
  • I profiled the core audience (men aged 18–39) and built cast profiles from Baidu Index and Weibo, showing how each lead could reach a different age group.

05What I took from it

Good insight work combines what people say (surveys) with what they do (viewing, search and review data), segments before it averages, and ends in a decision someone can act on. It is the same approach I use today for customer feedback, now with dashboards and AI.
