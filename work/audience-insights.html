Film & TV audience insights: from survey data to release strategy

As a marketing data analyst at CC-Smart's film market research centre in Beijing, I worked on audience research for studios and broadcasters: a national cinema-goer survey, a pre-release study for a major fantasy film, and a data-driven assessment of a TV drama before it went on air.

2017 – 2019
Role
Marketing Data Analyst
Clients
Film studios & broadcasters
Tools

01A national cinema survey

SSituation

The 2018 China Film Audience Survey, part of a big-data project on risk assessment for films and TV dramas funded by the Beijing Municipal Social Science Foundation, needed reliable nationwide data on who goes to the cinema and why.

TTask

Help build the questionnaire, run fieldwork across 54 cities and turn the data into findings for an industry report.

AAction
  • Drafted and revised the questionnaire: viewing habits, information channels, rating platforms and decision drivers.
  • Supervised in-cinema fieldwork in five city tiers (January 2018) with daily quality control: flagged speeders and careless answers and tracked valid responses per city.
  • Analysed audience behaviour and satisfaction in SPSS.
RResult
54cities covered
2,000+valid responses
Tier 1–2cities with the highest attendance
  • Tier-1 and Tier-2 audiences went more often and spent more, which challenged the assumption that growth lay in lower-tier cities and fed into the client's expansion and promotion plans.
  • Co-wrote analytical chapters of the published industry report and presented the findings.

02Pre-release study: Legend of the Demon Cat

SSituation

Three months before Chen Kaige's fantasy epic Legend of the Demon Cat (2017) opened, the studio needed to know who was interested, what drew them in and what could go wrong.

TTask

Design and analyse a pre-release audience study to guide the marketing plan.

AAction
  • Ran two offline questionnaires: one for general audiences (3,494 collected, 2,323 after weighting) and one for fans of the cast (656).
  • Used frequency, cross-tab and factor analysis in SPSS to compare fans with general audiences.
RResult
2,323general-audience respondents (weighted, from 3,494)
81.2%already knew the film
87.1%of those planned to see it in cinemas
  • Two audiences, two decisions: 93.2% of fans chose on the cast alone, 44 points more than general viewers, so the campaign needed two tracks.
  • A positioning risk: 65.7% saw the film as fantasy but only 22.4% as an art film, so I recommended making the genre clear in all materials.

03After release: a published case study

SSituation

Legend of the Demon Cat opened on 22 December 2017 to strong reviews and six Asian Film Awards nominations, yet finished 14th among Chinese films of the year with 529 million RMB.

TTask

Explain the gap between quality and box office in a case chapter for the Blue Book of China's Film and TV Industry (2018).

AAction
  • Broke the film down into six factors: source novel, cast, production, directing style, marketing and release window.
  • Combined review data (the source novel scored 7.6 on Douban from 1,800+ ratings) with production facts: 97% of scenes shot on a real Tang-dynasty set and 80% of the cat computer-generated.
  • Tracked daily box office and screen share against competing releases using Maoyan and Taopiaopiao data.
RResult
  • Quality won the audience: with the lowest screen share on opening day it had the highest occupancy (27.6%), and by day three it led its release cohort with 24.9% of screenings.
  • The calendar lost the market: it was squeezed between Youth and Ex-Files 3, which took over 50% of screenings, then hit by three waves of new releases (average screen share 10.57%, 4th).
  • The core tension: an art-house ambition and a fantasy story far from everyday life limited mass appeal, so art came before the market.
  • Published as chapter B.14 (pp. 251–264), co-authored with Chen Zixin.

04Pre-broadcast assessment: Abyss Walker

SSituation

Abyss Walker, a 42-episode undercover crime drama, was planned for Hunan TV's prime-time slot and online platforms.

TTask

Predict interest and reception before broadcast, and say what would help the series land.

AAction
1 · Market13 TV dramas & 14 web series in the genre
2 · Platform19 dramas in Hunan TV's prime-time slot
3 · Benchmark10 comparable titles, scored on topic, plot and cast
4 · AudienceDouban reviews, Baidu Index & Weibo data
RResult
  • The genre was scarce on TV (13 titles in 19 months) with decent reviews (average Douban 6.4) and room to grow (average rating 0.97).
  • Hunan TV's slot averaged a 2.87 rating but was dominated by romance and costume dramas, so a crime drama would stand out.
  • Review mining showed this audience punishes slow plots and unnecessary romance subplots, which became my main content recommendation.
  • Core audience: men aged 18–39; cast profiles showed how each lead could reach a different age group.

05What I delivered for clients

As a marketing data analyst, my job was to give studios, distributors and broadcasters evidence they could act on before money was spent. Across these projects I contributed in five ways:

  • Turned business questions into research. Designed questionnaires and samples for a 54-city national survey and for film-specific studies, so each client question had data behind it.
  • Protected data quality. Ran daily quality control across 54 cities, so analysis and client reports rested on clean, valid responses.
  • Segmented before recommending. Separated fans from general audiences and big cities from smaller ones, which changed the advice: a two-track film campaign and expansion focused on Tier-1 and Tier-2 cities.
  • Combined what people say with what they do. Matched survey answers with box office, screen share, Douban reviews, Baidu Index and Weibo data to check the story from several angles.
  • Delivered decision-ready output. Pre-launch recommendations on positioning, content and target audiences, plus analytical chapters in a national industry report and a published case study in the 2018 Blue Book.

The same approach still guides my work today: start from the decision, protect the data, segment the audience and finish with a clear recommendation, now with BI dashboards and AI.
